# Meteorology — Quick Reference

*Atmospheric values are approximate study aids; use the forecast and official briefing for flight decisions.*

## ISA, lapse rates, density

| Reference | Approximate value |
|---|---|
| ISA sea level | 15 °C; 1013.25 hPa; density 1.225 kg/m³ |
| Pressure change | 1 hPa ≈ 27 ft near the surface (rough approximation) |
| Dry adiabatic rate | 3 °C / 1,000 ft |
| ISA tropospheric rate | 2 °C / 1,000 ft |
| Saturated adiabatic rate | 1.5 °C / 1,000 ft (variable) |

\[
\rho \propto \frac{P}{T}\qquad(T\text{ in absolute units})
\]

Higher temperature, higher elevation, or lower pressure → lower density. A smaller temperature/dew-point spread means air is nearer saturation.

**Stability:** absolute stable → stable → conditionally unstable → unstable; compare environmental lapse rate with parcel cooling rate. An inversion (temperature rises with height) is strongly stable.

## Fog and fronts

| Type | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Radiation | Clear night + light wind + moist air + surface cooling |
| Advection | Moist air moving over a colder surface |
| Orographic | Moist air forced up rising terrain |
| Steaming | Cold air over warmer water adds moisture |
| Frontal | Precipitation evaporates into cooler air below |

| Warm front | Cold front |
|---|---|
| Gentle lift; broad layered cloud | Steeper lift; narrower convective band |
| Prolonged precipitation, low cloud/poor visibility | Showers, turbulence, possible CB/hail |
| Temperature tends to rise after passage | Temperature tends to fall after passage |

Northern Hemisphere memory aid: flow circulates counterclockwise around a low and clockwise around a high; surface friction modifies the flow.

## Hazards and products

- Thunderstorms: strong up/down drafts, turbulence, hail, icing, lightning; avoid, do not penetrate.
- Icing requires visible moisture and sub-zero conditions; clear ice often comes from larger droplets, rime from smaller droplets.
- Decode METAR/TAF as a whole: wind → visibility/weather → cloud → temperature/dew point → pressure/trends.
- Use charts, radar, satellite, SIGMET/AIRMET and pilot reports together; radar precipitation is not a guarantee of safe air.
