# Air Law — PPL(A) exam cheat sheet

**Use this as an exam-revision aid, not as an operational substitute for current SERA, the Belgian AIP, NOTAMs, or instructor guidance.** Where the question bank's keyed answer conflicts with a general rule or the lesson slide, the bank answer is shown and the discrepancy is flagged. Operational rules and local values can change.

## 1. Legal framework, organisations and documents (Q1–10, 66–69)

### Who makes which rules?

| Layer / body | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Chicago Convention | Signed 7 Dec 1944; foundation of ICAO. ICAO came into operation with the Convention in 1947. A State controls the airspace over its territory and territorial waters. |
| ICAO | UN specialised agency; global civil aviation standards and recommended practices (SARPs) are in Annexes. Annex 1 = Personnel Licensing. ICAO Annexes are not automatically identical to domestic law: states implement them, including differences/notified differences. |
| EASA / EU | EASA prepares/supports aviation regulation; EU regulations are legally binding in Member States without separate national ratification. EASA Easy Access Rules consolidate the regulation and AMC/GM. |
| NPA → AMC/GM → EU Regulation | NPA = Notice of Proposed Amendment (consultation); AMC = Acceptable Means of Compliance; GM = Guidance Material; a regulation is adopted through EU institutions and is binding. AMC/GM explain acceptable compliance/guidance; they are not themselves the regulation. |
| SERA | Standardised European Rules of the Air; the main European rules-of-the-air source for this syllabus; largely based on ICAO. Intended to support a Single European Sky. |
| EUROCONTROL | Coordinates European network/flow-management functions; lesson example: high-level ATC above FL245 over Benelux and parts of Germany. |
| Belgium | FOD Mobiliteit has federal civil-aviation competence; DGLV (Directorate-General for Civil Aviation) is the competent authority/CAA; Skeyes is the national ANSP. Regional governments operate/equip/maintain regional airports. Belgian rules supplement EU rules; lesson notes OO-registered aircraft remain subject to Belgian rules abroad. |

**Memory hook:** *ICAO = worldwide standards; EASA/EU = European binding regulation; DGLV = Belgian oversight; Skeyes = air navigation services.*

### Aircraft / pilot status and papers

| Item | Exam recall / use |
|---|---|
| ARC | Airworthiness Review Certificate (Airworthiness Review Certificate); confirms the review, not the aircraft's registration. |
| Certificate of Airworthiness (CofA) | Issued by the State of Registry; bank answer for validity is **unlimited** (subject to continued airworthiness/valid review). |
| International flight documents (Q5) | Bank's keyed combination is **a, b, c, e, f**: registration certificate, CofA, ARC, aircraft logbook, and the specified "appropriate papers…" item. EASA Form 1 is not one of the selected onboard papers in this question. The bank stem is clipped after “appropriate papers for eve…”, so use the keyed combination rather than guessing the missing wording. |
| National flag | Bank key: carried/displayed **on every flight**. |
| Class 2 medical at age 62 | **12 months** (bank key). |
| Student first solo, aeroplane/helicopter | Minimum **16 years**. |
| PPL validity | Licence validity period is **unlimited**; exercise of privileges still depends on valid medical, ratings, recency and applicable rules. |
| PPL privileges (bank wording) | PIC on non-commercial flights without remuneration; remuneration may be received as a flight instructor only when separately qualified/authorised. A PPL alone is not a commercial-pilot privilege. |
| SEP(land) renewal | If the required experience route is not met, bank answer: proficiency check with an examiner. Distinguish renewal from revalidation by experience. |
| ICAO licence | Bank answer: valid in all ICAO countries (subject to recognition/validation and each State's rules in practice). |

**Exam trap:** A certificate's *validity* is not the same as the ongoing validity of the aircraft's airworthiness status or of the pilot's right to exercise privileges.

## 2. Flight definitions, day/night and minimum height (Q11–14, 24–25, 71–72)

| Term / rule | Meaning / exam answer |
|---|---|
| VFR flight | A flight **conducted under Visual Flight Rules**. It is not defined simply by having good visibility or being in VMC. |
| IFR | Instrument Flight Rules. |
| VMC | Visual Meteorological Conditions: weather meeting the applicable visibility/cloud minima. VMC describes conditions; VFR describes rules. |
| Ceiling | Height above ground/water of the base of the lowest cloud layer covering **more than half** the sky, below **20,000 ft**. |
| Civil twilight | Sun's centre is at most **6° below** the horizon: morning civil twilight ends at sunrise; evening civil twilight starts at sunset. Lesson notes distinguish night logging/rules as after evening civil twilight to before morning civil twilight; check local legal definition. |
| Minimum height | Do not fly below prescribed minimum heights except for permitted necessities such as take-off/landing or the stated authorised tasks. The bank says **bad weather near an aerodrome is not an exception**. Do not fly underneath a bridge or similar structure. |
| Bridge / similar structure | Bank key: **never fly below it**; the distractor's 150 m AGL does not create an exception. |
| Special VFR | Requires the applicable ATC clearance/conditions; bank's tested condition is **visual reference to terrain**. Do not confuse SVFR with ordinary VFR minima. |
| Night VFR rating | Bank key: **5 h** additional night flight; **3 h** with instructor (including at least **1 h** cross-country); **5** solo take-offs and full-stop landings. |

**How to answer definition questions:** identify whether the stem asks about *rules* (VFR/IFR), *weather* (VMC/IMC), *height* (AGL/AMSL), or a *time boundary* (sunrise/sunset/civil twilight). These terms are not interchangeable.

## 3. VFR minima and airspace (Q15–21, 34, 70, 72)

### Bank-tested visibility and cloud minima

| Question condition | Bank answer |
|---|---:|
| VFR in Class C below FL100 | Flight visibility **5 km** |
| VFR in Class E at FL75 | Flight visibility **5 km** |
| VFR in Class C at FL110 or FL125 | Flight visibility **8 km** |
| VFR in Class B at 5,000 ft MSL | Flight visibility **5 km** |
| VFR cloud distance in C/D/E (bank question) | **1,500 m horizontal, 1,000 ft vertical** |
| VFR cloud distance in B (bank question) | **1,500 m horizontal, 300 m vertical** (bank-keyed value) |

**How to use:** first read the *airspace class*, then the *altitude band*; choose the visibility for that band; finally apply the cloud-clearance rule separately. Do not substitute ground visibility for flight visibility.

**Unit note:** the bank gives the Class B vertical distance as **300 m**, while the lesson gives **1,000 ft** for the standard cloud separation. These are equivalent to normal exam rounding (1,000 ft ≈ 305 m); use the units and keyed choice shown in the question. Verify current SERA before operations.

### Class quick map

| Class | VFR entry / service headline | Separation headline to remember |
|---|---|---|
| A | VFR not permitted. | — |
| B | Clearance required. | ATC separation from all aircraft. |
| C | Clearance/radio procedures apply. | IFR separated from IFR and VFR; VFR separated from IFR; traffic information between VFR. |
| D | Clearance/radio procedures apply. | IFR separated from IFR; traffic information; VFR not separated from VFR. |
| E | VFR generally does not require ATC clearance (follow published procedures). | VFR receives no separation from other traffic (bank Q34). |
| F | Advisory/uncontrolled as designated. | No ATC separation for VFR. |
| G | Uncontrolled. | No ATC separation for VFR; pilot maintains lookout and collision avoidance. |

**Exam traps:** “VFR not separated” does not mean “no traffic information”; “clearance” is not the same as “radio contact”; check the vertical limits printed on the chart.

## 4. Right of way and collision avoidance (Q22–23, 73–75, 79)

```text
Head-on / nearly head-on → BOTH alter course to the RIGHT.
Converging at same level → aircraft that has the other on its RIGHT gives way.
Overtaking → overtaking aircraft keeps clear and passes well clear.
```

- Aircraft on the right has priority in a crossing situation; the other aircraft gives way. The bank's Q22/Q74 wording (“from right to left”) refers to which aircraft is on the other pilot's right—reason from the *relative position*, not left/right labels in isolation.
- When two engine-driven aircraft meet head-on, both turn right (Q23/Q75).
- A powered aircraft gives way to aircraft in higher-priority categories (airship, glider, balloon) and to an aircraft towing another aircraft/object. Lesson's hierarchy reflects ability/speed to manoeuvre: balloon > glider > airship > ordinary powered aircraft, with towing protected.
- Landing aircraft: do not cut in front of an aircraft established on final/landing; lower aircraft must not exploit priority to cut in.
- Wake-turbulence phrase: **“Caution wake turbulence.”**

**Important bank inconsistency (Q73):** the bank keys “both have to divert to the right” for two engine-driven aircraft on crossing tracks. The ordinary crossing rule is that the aircraft with the other on its right gives way; it does not normally mean both turn. Memorise the bank's marked option for that exact stem, but understand/apply the standard give-way rule in real operations and reconcile with your instructor/current SERA.

## 5. Altimetry and cruising levels (Q28–29, 76)

| Setting / term | Use |
|---|---|
| QNH | Altimeter indicates altitude above mean sea level (AMSL) when on the ground at the aerodrome. |
| QFE | Altimeter indicates height above the selected aerodrome datum. |
| 1013.25 hPa | Standard pressure setting; altitudes are expressed as flight levels (FL). At FL80 set **1013.25 hPa**. |
| Transition altitude (TA) | On climb, change from QNH to **1013.25 hPa when climbing above TA**. |
| Transition level (TL) | On descent, change from standard pressure to local QNH when descending below the published TL. The transition layer lies between TA and TL; TA/TL are published, not universal guesses. |
| Semi-circular rule | Reduces opposing traffic at the same level; select by **magnetic track/course**, not heading. Follow ATC clearance and published route/airspace exceptions. |

**Semi-circular memory:** *East 000°–179° = odd; West 180°–359° = even.* For VFR cruising levels above the applicable threshold, add **500 ft** (odd + 500 eastbound; even + 500 westbound). For IFR, use the appropriate full flight levels without the VFR +500. Lesson sketch examples: east FL50/70/90/110; west FL60/80/100/120. Always apply the legally published scheme for the airspace and altitude.

**Worked example — pressure setting:** aircraft climbing through a published TA from QNH 1004 hPa: above TA set **1013.25 hPa** and report/maintain the cleared flight level. On descent, retain standard setting until the published TL, then set the latest local QNH. Never infer a TA/TL from the question unless supplied.

## 6. ATS, airspace structures and transponder (Q30–40, 70–81)

### Air traffic services and airspace

| Term | Full name / recall |
|---|---|
| ATC | Air Traffic Control; responsible for control/separation and safe orderly conduct of flights within its remit. ATC units: **TWR** (aerodrome), **APP** (approach), **ACC** (area control). |
| FIS | Flight Information Service; pilot contacts by **radio**. Provides useful information, not an ATC separation/clearance guarantee. |
| ALRS / ALR | Alerting service. A FIR may provide **FIS + alerting service** (bank answer). |
| AIS | Aeronautical Information Service; publishes aeronautical information (AIP, NOTAM products), not ATC. |
| FIR | Flight Information Region: defined region in which flight information and alerting services are provided; not itself an airspace class. |
| AWY | Airway, a published route/corridor with defined vertical limits and class; chart/vertical cross-section matters. |
| CTR / TMA / CTA | Control zone / terminal manoeuvring area / control area; controlled airspace boundaries and bases/tops are three-dimensional. Read horizontal chart limits and vertical limits together. |
| RMZ | Radio Mandatory Zone: continuously monitor the published frequency and, if possible, establish two-way communication. It is not an automatic ATC-clearance requirement. |
| TMZ | Transponder Mandatory Zone: before entry select the required transponder, typically **Mode A+C or Mode S** as published; it is not itself a clearance. |
| TRA | Temporary Reserved Airspace. Read active times, vertical limits and conditions in AIP/NOTAM. |

### SSR / transponder

| Mode / code | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mode A | Transmits assigned identity/code. |
| Mode C | Adds pressure-altitude reporting. |
| Mode S | Selective addressing; can transmit altitude and aircraft identity/data. |
| 7000 | Common VFR conspicuity code where prescribed. |
| 7500 | Unlawful interference. |
| 7600 | Radio communication failure. |
| 7700 | Emergency; select without request when required by the emergency procedure (bank answer). |
| 2000 | Common general/IFR code in some contexts; use only as directed/published. |

**Mnemonic:** *75 hijack, 76 radio, 77 emergency.* Do not mix up Mode A/C/S (equipment modes) with squawk codes.

### Position reports and wake

- Position report format illustrated by the bank: **callsign – position – altitude/level**, e.g. “DEABC, N, 2500 ft.” Use the full required report elements/frequency for the applicable service.
- **AIREP** = Aircraft Report.
- “Caution wake turbulence” is the standard warning phrase tested by the bank.

## 7. AIP, NOTAM, PIB and restricted airspace (Q41–45, 65, 82)

### AIP structure

| AIP part | Contents / recall |
|---|---|
| GEN | General information: chart symbols, radio-navigation aids, sunrise/sunset, charges/fees and general rules. |
| ENR | En-route: airspace, ATS routes, procedures, navigation and restrictions/warnings. |
| AD | Aerodromes: aerodrome information, charts, approach/taxi information. |

**Memory hook:** *GENeral → ENRoute → Aerodromes.* The AIP has **GEN, ENR, AD**.

- Look up the **type/conditions of a restricted area in the AIP** (bank answer). Check current NOTAMs for temporary activation/status.
- **Prohibited**: entry forbidden; **restricted**: entry only under stated restrictions/permission; **danger**: activity may be hazardous—avoid or comply with published conditions. A restricted area is the tested “may be crossed with restrictions” answer.
- **PIB** (Pre-flight Information Bulletin): current operationally significant **NOTAM** information assembled before flight.

### Decode the bank NOTAM

Example: `A1024/13 A) LOWW B) 1305211200 C) 1305211400 E) STOCKERAU VOR STO 113.00 UNSERVICEABLE`

| Field | Read it as |
|---|---|
| `A1024/13` | NOTAM series/number/year. |
| `A)` | Location/affected aerodrome or FIR: LOWW. |
| `B)` | Start: 2013-05-21 **12:00 UTC** (`YYMMDDHHMM`). |
| `C)` | End: 2013-05-21 **14:00 UTC**; question asks validity *until* this end time. |
| `E)` | Plain-language operational content: VOR STO 113.00 unserviceable at Stockerau. |

**Worked example — date/time:** `1305211400` = year 2013, month 05, day 21, 14:00 UTC. Do not read the first two digits as the day; NOTAM times are UTC unless the text explicitly says otherwise.

## 8. Aerodrome operations, signs, markings and lights (Q46–61, 83–92)

### Definitions and visual signals

| Item | Recognition / answer |
|---|---|
| Runway | Rectangular area on a **land** aerodrome prepared for landing and take-off of aircraft. |
| Aerodrome elevation | Highest point of the landing area. |
| Taxi holding point | Point at which aircraft must stop unless permission to proceed is given. A taxi clearance does not automatically cancel a runway holding instruction. |
| Signal square | Ground area with symbols for visually informing overflying aircraft of aerodrome conditions; not a place to taxi for clearances. |
| Landing-direction indicator | **T-shaped** indicator. |
| Wind-direction indicator | Windsock/cone shows the direction **from which** wind comes; a surrounding white circle improves visibility. |
| Aerodrome beacon (ABN) | Rotating beacon at an aerodrome indicating its location to pilots **from the air**. |
| Runway designators | Runway magnetic direction rounded to nearest 10° and expressed in two digits; parallel runways add L/C/R. For two parallels, left = L, right = R (e.g. 06L / 06R). |
| Runway paint / lights | Runway markings **white**; runway edge lights **white**; runway end lights **red**. Taxiway signs/markings are generally yellow. |
| Uniform longitudinal bars about runway centreline (bank phrasing) | Do not touch down before them (bank answer). Identify the marking by its position/pattern in the figure, not merely its colour. |
| PAPI | Visual aid that helps the pilot acquire/maintain the correct approach glidepath. Four-light indications: **4 red = too low; 3 red/1 white = slightly low; 2 red/2 white = on path; 1 red/3 white = slightly high; 4 white = too high.** |

### Read the bank's figures

- **ALW-010, yellow X on red:** landing prohibited for a longer period. This is an aerodrome signal, not a runway designation.
- **ALW-011, white cross on dark/contrasting field:** glider flying is in progress. Treat it as a signal-square visual warning.
- **ALW-019, black sign with yellow “A”:** taxiway A (location sign). Read the letter inside the black panel; do not call it “point A” or a runway.
- **ALW-020, yellow panel with left arrow + “2500 m”:** from this intersection, **2,500 m of runway is available in the arrow's direction**. Follow arrow direction; it is not automatically total runway length or distance to parking.
- **Threshold / touchdown markings:** locate the threshold and centreline first; count/identify the symmetrical bars. In this bank's wording, the tested stripes mean do not touch down before them.
- **PAPI:** compare red vs white lamps: more red = below slope; more white = above; equal pairs = on slope.

### Runway surface condition (bank/lesson RCR)

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| DRY | No visible moisture/contamination. |
| DAMP | Moisture changes the surface colour. |
| WET | Surface soaked, but no significant standing-water patches. |
| WATER PATCHES | Visible patches of standing water. |
| Standing water | Standing water depth **over 3 mm** in the RCR matrix. |
| Runway Condition Report (RCR) | Reports runway condition for each third; communicated in relevant ATS/AIS information. Matrix codes: **6 dry, 5 good, 4 good-to-medium, 3 medium, 2 medium-to-poor, 1 poor, 0 less-than-poor**. Use aircraft performance data and current report, not this memory table alone. |

### Aerodrome light-gun signals

| Signal to aircraft | In flight | On ground |
|---|---|---|
| Steady green | Cleared to land | Cleared for take-off |
| Flashing green | Return for landing (then steady green when appropriate) | Cleared to taxi |
| Steady red | Give way to other aircraft and continue circling | Stop |
| Flashing red | Aerodrome unsafe; do not land | Vacate runway in use immediately |
| Flashing white | — | Return to starting point on aerodrome |
| Alternating red/green | Exercise extreme caution | Exercise extreme caution |

Acknowledge light signals **by rocking the wings in daytime** (bank answers for light signals and SAR ground signals). If visibility/day-night context is specified, use the prescribed acknowledgement for that context.

## 9. Interception, emergency, search and rescue, occurrence investigation (Q26–27, 62–64, 87, 93–94)

### Interception signals in the bank

| Interceptor action | Meaning tested |
|---|---|
| Alternating aileron movement (normally left of intercepted aircraft), then smooth turn left | **Follow me.** |
| Abrupt heading change of 90° or more and pull-up without crossing intercepted aircraft's track | **You may continue your flight.** |

Follow the applicable published intercept procedures, try to establish radio contact on the designated frequency (including 121.5 MHz where appropriate), and comply with unmistakable instructions. Do not confuse the two manoeuvre/signal descriptions.

### Emergency and SAR

| Term / action | Recall |
|---|---|
| INCERFA | Uncertainty phase. |
| ALERFA | Alert phase. |
| DETRESFA | Distress phase. |
| Ground-to-air SAR signal acknowledgement | Rock the wings. |
| SAR service | Can be provided by both civil and military organisations. |
| Occurrence categories | Incident → serious incident → accident (three categories). |
| Accident investigation purpose | Find causes and improve safety / make recommendations; **not** to assign guilt, civil liability or criminal blame. |

## 10. Exam traps, quick checks and question coverage

### Traps to spot

1. **VFR vs VMC:** rules vs weather conditions.
2. **MSL/AMSL vs AGL vs flight level:** identify the reference before choosing an altitude.
3. **“NOT correct” / “NOT sufficient”:** Q14 asks which is *not* an exception; bad weather is not sufficient.
4. **Traffic information vs separation; clearance vs instruction:** information is not a separation guarantee; a clearance is permission, an instruction is to be followed.
5. **AIP vs NOTAM vs PIB:** permanent/general publication vs current notice vs a pre-flight bundle of NOTAM information.
6. **RMZ vs TMZ:** radio monitoring/contact vs transponder equipment; neither term alone means “obtain clearance.”
7. **Squawk codes:** 7500 unlawful interference, 7600 radio failure, 7700 emergency.
8. **Runway distance signs:** read arrow direction and whether the value is available runway from that intersection.
9. **QNH vs 1013.25:** QNH below TA; standard pressure above TA / at flight levels.
10. **Read the exact altitude boundary:** below FL100 is not the same wording as above FL100.
11. **Bank-keyed quirk:** Q73 says both crossing engine-driven aircraft turn right. See the flagged note above.
12. **Multiple-choice “which is NOT correct”:** reject distractors even if they sound operationally plausible; don't assume “commercial aircraft” automatically has priority over a light aircraft.
