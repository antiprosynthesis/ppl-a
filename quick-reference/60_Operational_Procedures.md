# Operational Procedures — Quick Reference

*For aircraft-specific actions, use the checklist and POH/AFM.*

## Wind shear and wake turbulence

- Wind shear = rapid change in wind speed and/or direction over a short distance.
- Microbursts can cause a sudden headwind gain followed by a severe loss/downdraft; avoid known cells and follow the published escape procedure.
- Wake vortices strongest behind aircraft that are **heavy, clean, slow**.
- Vortices descend and drift with wind; avoid the preceding aircraft's flight path, stay above it where possible, and apply published spacing.

## Abnormal and emergency actions

```text
Fly the aircraft → identify the problem → use the correct checklist
Communicate / choose a safe landing option → brief occupants if time allows
```

- Smoke/fire responses differ for electrical, engine, and cabin sources; do not improvise a single sequence.
- Emergency landing: forced by immediate circumstances. Precautionary landing: chosen before the situation is critical.
- Ditching: brief occupants; inflate life vest after exiting.
- Noise abatement must never compromise safe control or required obstacle clearance.

## Runway and landing hazards

| Condition | Main concern |
|---|---|
| Wet/standing water | Reduced braking, spray, hydroplaning |
| Snow/slush/ice | Contamination, poor acceleration/braking, directional control |
| Wet grass/soft surface | Increased take-off/landing distance |
| Hot brakes | Fire/tyre hazard; approach cautiously and use checklist |

Use approved performance data for actual runway state, wind, slope, mass, and temperature.
