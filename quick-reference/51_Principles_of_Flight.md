# Principles of Flight (Aeroplane) — Quick Reference

*The POH/AFM controls aircraft-specific speeds, limitations, and recovery procedures.*

## Forces and drag

\[
q=\tfrac12\rho V^2,\qquad L=qSC_L,\qquad D=qSC_D
\]

| Drag | Strongest tendency |
|---|---|
| Induced | High lift demand / low speed |
| Parasite | High speed; rises approximately with \(V^2\) |
| Total | Induced + parasite + other contributions |

## Stall and turn

- Angle of attack = chord line ↔ relative airflow; it is not pitch attitude.
- Stall = critical angle of attack exceeded, at any speed/configuration.
- Load factor \(n=L/W\). In a coordinated, level turn: \(n=1/\cos\phi\).
- Same configuration: \(V_{S,\text{turn}}\approx V_{S,1g}\sqrt{n}\).
- Wing loading \(=W/S\); higher loading generally means higher required lift coefficient/speed for the same conditions.
- Stall recovery starts by reducing angle of attack; then follow the aircraft procedure.

## Controls and design

| Axis | Motion | Primary control |
|---|---|---|
| Longitudinal (nose-tail) | Roll | Ailerons |
| Lateral (wingtip-wingtip) | Pitch | Elevator |
| Vertical | Yaw | Rudder |

| Feature | Quick effect |
|---|---|
| Flaps | More lift and drag; take-off/landing effect depends on setting |
| Slat/slot | Delays separation; raises usable angle of attack |
| Washout/stall strip | Root stalls first; helps retain tip/aileron control |
| Dihedral | Lateral stability in sideslip |
| Differential/Frise aileron | Reduces adverse yaw |
| Ground effect | Reduces induced drag near surface |

```text
Low speed/high lift → induced drag ↑     High speed → parasite drag ↑
```
