# Communication — Quick Reference

*Use standard phraseology and the current local radio-failure procedure.*

## Priority and plain language

| Call | Meaning |
|---|---|
| MAYDAY | Distress: grave and imminent danger |
| PAN PAN | Urgency: urgent safety concern, not immediate distress |
| ROGER | I received your last transmission |
| WILCO | I understand and will comply |
| AFFIRM / NEGATIVE | Yes / no |

**Roger is not “yes.”** Read back required clearances and safety-critical instructions; acknowledgement is not a clearance.

## Readback and radio technique

- Read back runway, altitude/level, heading, route, transponder code, and frequency as required.
- Wind and traffic information generally do not require readback; follow local phraseology.
- Listen before transmitting; use the full call sign initially and standard phonetics/numerals.
- Readability scale: **1 unreadable → 5 perfectly readable**.
- Blind transmission: use when unable to establish contact but a transmission is necessary; use the prescribed frequency and format.

## Frequencies and codes

| Item | Recall |
|---|---|
| Aeronautical VHF voice band | 118.000–136.975 MHz |
| 7500 / 7600 / 7700 | Unlawful interference / radio failure / emergency |
| QDM | Magnetic bearing to station |
| QDR | Magnetic bearing from station |
| QTE | True bearing from station |

Approximate radio horizon from Notes: \(R\text{ (NM)}\approx1.23\sqrt{h\text{ (ft)}}\), with a rough 10% allowance sometimes applied. Actual range depends on both antenna heights, terrain, and service.

## Distress/urgency call essentials

```text
Who you call → MAYDAY/PAN PAN (as appropriate) → station/callsign
Your callsign/type → nature of problem → intentions/position/altitude
Assistance required → other useful information
```

Use the applicable emergency frequency/procedure; do not delay an urgent call to make it perfect.
