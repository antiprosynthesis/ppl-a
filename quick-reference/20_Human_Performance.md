# Human Performance — Quick Reference

*Recognise impairment early. Use the checklist, seek assistance, and land when required.*

## Oxygen and pressure

| Fact | Recall |
|---|---|
| Oxygen in air | About 21% at all normal flight altitudes |
| Altitude effect | Pressure and oxygen partial pressure fall with height |
| Hypoxia | Impaired judgement/vision, euphoria, fatigue; insight may be lost |
| CO poisoning | Odourless; headache, nausea, drowsiness, confusion |

```text
Suspected hypoxia → descend + oxygen as appropriate → land / get help
Suspected CO → cabin heat off + fresh air/oxygen → land promptly per POH
```

## Distinguish common conditions

| Condition | Clue |
|---|---|
| Hyperventilation | Over-breathing; tingling, dizziness, spasm; reduced CO₂ |
| Barotrauma | Ear/sinus pain when pressure cannot equalise |
| Decompression sickness | Nitrogen bubbles after pressure reduction; risk after diving |
| Spatial disorientation | Vestibular illusion; trust instruments over false sensation |
| Positive G | Grey-out → tunnel vision/black-out |
| Negative G | Red-out |

If uncertain whether symptoms are hypoxia or hyperventilation, treat suspected hypoxia first.

## Human factors scan

- Stress, fatigue, illness, alcohol, and medication can degrade performance.
- **Active error:** action at the controls. **Latent error:** hidden weakness in planning, procedures, or organisation.
- Use a disciplined instrument scan when visual cues or the vestibular system may mislead.
- Pause: **Am I fit? What changed? What is the safest simple action?**
